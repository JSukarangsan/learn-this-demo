# Learn.this — H2 2026 Planning (summary)

Owner: VP Product · Audience: all product teams · Refresh: quarterly, at half planning.

## Where we are

H1 closed at 12,400 active learners and an 86% blended completion rate. Both are up, and
neither is as good as it looks. Completion is dominated by cohort mix — one large org
cohort moved the company number 3 points on its own in May — and growth came almost
entirely from assigned seats, not self-serve. Instructor supply hasn't moved in 18
months; every downstream constraint (schedule density, catalog breadth, time to first
session) traces back to it.

## The H2 bet

Reduce the cost of running a cohort, for the instructor and for us. Not more courses, not
more marketing — the same instructors running more cohorts with less staff involved per
one. Three things follow:

1. **Self-service scheduling.** Ops enters ~40 schedules/month by hand and gets ~3 wrong.
   Each error becomes a support thread with an entire cohort.
2. **A catalog people can search.** Multi-word queries return poor results — mostly a
   titling problem, not a ranking problem, so the fix sits with Instructor Tools' content
   model, not with search.
3. **Recorded sessions that work.** Top complaint from learners who miss a live session;
   every fix plan runs into a pinned vendor SDK.

## Targets

| Metric | Target | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Schedules entered by hand | 40/mo → 0 | Zero for two consecutive weeks is the bar |
| Enrollment completion | 82% → 88% | Mobile is where the loss is |
| Completion rate | +4 pts | Segmented — the blended number moves on cohort mix alone |
| Time to first session | Reduce the controllable part | Floor is set by the cohort calendar, not by us |

## Guardrails — not traded for velocity

- **Live session reliability.** A dropped live session is unrecoverable.
- **320px.** ~40% of live attendance is mobile; a regression at the smallest width is
  invisible in internal testing.
- **Refund volume.** Every mid-cohort enrollment change is a manual refund; Finance
  absorbs the difference.
- **Support tickets per cohort.** The cheapest early-warning signal — moves before any
  other metric does.

## What we said no to, and why

- **A mobile app.** Attendance is mobile, authoring isn't — an app solves the half of
  the problem already adequate.
- **Certificates and accreditation.** Asked for in every enterprise deal; it's a
  compliance product, not a learning product, and not staffed for.
- **Personalized ranking.** Not enough behavioral data to do it well, and a bad
  personalized ranker is worse than a plain one.
- **Async-only cohorts.** "That is a different company. Live is the product."

## How the teams divide

Webapp owns what a learner touches — catalog, enrollment, live session room, recorded
player. Instructor Tools owns course creation and the content model. Growth owns
acquisition and lifecycle. Billing owns the org admin console and refunds. The biggest
source of confusion is the content model: it's Instructor Tools' call even when the
symptom shows up in Webapp's surface area, and three of the four H2 bets touch it.

## Open questions for the half

- Do recordings stay with the vendor or move to owned storage? The cost model differs by
  roughly an order of magnitude either way, and nobody has done the arithmetic yet.
- What's the right pricing unit once org seats outpace self-serve? Seats and learners
  have diverged far enough that Billing and Insights already report different numbers
  for the same month — both correctly.
